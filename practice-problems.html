Chapter 16 — Kinetics
P1Reaction Rate from Stoichiometry

Dinitrogen pentoxide decomposes: 2N2O5(g) → 4NO2(g) + O2(g). In a sealed flask, [N2O5] drops from 0.500 M to 0.480 M over 20.0 s.

(a) What is the rate of disappearance of N2O5? (b) What is the rate of the reaction? (c) At what rate is O2 forming? (d) At what rate is NO2 forming?

P2Method of Initial Rates

For the reaction 2A + B → products, three trials give the following initial rate data:

Trial[A] (M)[B] (M)Initial rate (M/s)
10.200.104.0×10−3
20.400.101.6×10−2
30.200.208.0×10−3

Determine (a) the order with respect to A, (b) the order with respect to B, (c) the overall reaction order, (d) the rate law, and (e) the value of k, with correct units.

P3First-Order Integrated Rate Law & Half-Life

A reaction is first order in A, with rate constant k = 0.0410 min−1 and [A]0 = 0.640 M.

(a) What is [A] after 15.0 minutes? (b) What is the half-life of this reaction?

P4Second-Order Integrated Rate Law & Half-Life

A reaction is second order in A, with rate constant k = 0.0360 L/mol·min and [A]0 = 0.250 M.

(a) What is [A] after 40.0 minutes? (b) What is the half-life of this reaction?

P5Zero-Order Half-Life Pattern

A reaction is zero order in A, with rate constant k = 0.0220 M/s and [A]0 = 0.880 M.

(a) What is the first half-life (time to go from 0.880 M to 0.440 M)? (b) What is the second half-life (time to go from 0.440 M to 0.220 M)? (c) How does the second half-life compare to the first, and why?

P6Arrhenius Equation — Solving for Ea

A reaction's rate constant is measured at two temperatures: k1 = 2.0×10−3 at 295 K, and k2 = 8.9×10−2 at 350 K.

Calculate the activation energy, Ea, in kJ/mol.

P7Arrhenius Equation — Solving for k at a New Temperature

A reaction has Ea = 88.0 kJ/mol. Its rate constant is 5.0×10−4 at 310 K.

What is the rate constant at 340 K?

P8Reaction Mechanism Analysis

The decomposition of nitryl chloride is proposed to occur by the following two-step mechanism:

Step 1 (slow): NO2Cl(g) → NO2(g) + Cl(g), rate constant k1
Step 2 (fast): NO2Cl(g) + Cl(g) → NO2(g) + Cl2(g), rate constant k2

(a) Write the overall balanced equation. (b) Identify the molecularity of each step. (c) Identify the reaction intermediate. (d) What is the experimentally observed rate law for the overall reaction?

P9Reaction Energy Diagram

A certain reaction has Ea(forward) = 62 kJ/mol and ΔHrxn = −35 kJ/mol.

(a) Is the reaction exothermic or endothermic? (b) Calculate Ea(reverse). (c) Which is larger, Ea(forward) or Ea(reverse)? Does that make sense given the sign of ΔHrxn?

Chapter 17 — Equilibrium
P10Writing K Expressions & Interpreting Magnitude

(a) Write the Kc expression for 2NO(g) + O2(g) ⇆ 2NO2(g). Given Kc = 6.0×105 at 25°C, does this equilibrium favor reactants or products?

(b) Write the Kc expression for the heterogeneous equilibrium Ag2CO3(s) ⇆ 2Ag+(aq) + CO32−(aq).

P11Reaction Quotient (Q vs. K)

At a certain temperature, K = 170 for 2NO2(g) ⇆ N2O4(g). At some point during the reaction, [NO2] = 0.025 M and [N2O4] = 0.040 M.

Calculate Q. Is the reaction at equilibrium? If not, which direction will it proceed?

P12Converting Kc to Kp

At 1000 K, Kc = 4.08×10−3 for 2SO3(g) ⇆ 2SO2(g) + O2(g).

Calculate Kp at this temperature.

P13ICE Table — Perfect-Square Shortcut

H2(g) + Br2(g) ⇆ 2HBr(g), K = 81.0. A 3.00 L flask is filled with 1.20 mol H2 and 1.20 mol Br2 (no HBr initially).

Set up an ICE table and find the equilibrium concentration of each species.

P14ICE Table — Small-x Approximation

2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇆ 2SO3(g), Kp = 1.0×10−3 at a certain temperature. A vessel is filled with PSO2 = 4.0 atm and PO2 = 2.5 atm (no SO3 initially).

(a) Using the small-x approximation, estimate the equilibrium partial pressure of SO3. (b) Check whether the approximation was valid.

P15Calculating Kp from Equilibrium Pressures

N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇆ 2NH3(g). At equilibrium: PN2 = 0.50 atm, PH2 = 0.75 atm, PNH3 = 0.20 atm.

Calculate Kp for this reaction.

P16Le Châtelier's Principle — Multiple Reactions

For each equilibrium below, predict whether the equilibrium shifts left, shifts right, or does not shift when the container's volume is decreased (pressure increased):

(a)2NO(g) + O2(g) ⇆ 2NO2(g)
(b)H2(g) + CO2(g) ⇆ H2O(g) + CO(g)
(c)N2O4(g) ⇆ 2NO2(g)
(d)CaCO3(s) ⇆ CaO(s) + CO2(g)

(e) The reaction 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇆ 2SO3(g) is exothermic. If temperature is raised, which way does equilibrium shift, and does K increase or decrease?
